Light, heat and speed: try the optogenetics toy model

Optogenetics puts light-gated switches into nerve cells. Shine the right colour and the cell fires. The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to this idea, and this page goes with our explainer videos about it.

Three questions. For each one, make a guess first. Then reveal what a small computer model says. All answers come from numbers the model worked out in advance; nothing is computed live and nothing is sent anywhere.

Step 1 of 3

Which colour reaches deepest into the brain?

A thin glass fibre (0.2 mm wide) shines 10 mW of light into brain tissue. The tissue scatters and absorbs it.

Step 2 of 3

How warm does the tissue get?

Light that is absorbed turns into heat. Here the light is blue (470 nm) and runs for 10 seconds. You can pulse it: the duty cycle is the share of time the light is on.

Step 3 of 3

Can the neuron keep up?

Flash the light in short pulses (2 ms each). The cell keeps up if at least 95 % of the pulses each give exactly one spike. Different light switches close at different speeds.

Make a guess for all three switches first.

The trade-off at a glance

In this toy model, colour sets reach and heat; closing speed sets following.

Reach and heat, per colour

Volume lit above 3 mW/mm² and warming are per mW of light out of the fibre. Reach and heat depend only on colour here: every switch shares ChR2's light sensitivity and one 3 mW/mm² threshold. The 590 nm and 635 nm advantages come only from wavelength, and absorption outside blood is not modelled, so they look better than they are (635 nm: a toy upper bound).

What is real and what is a toy

Real (taken from published sources, listed in the simulator's SOURCES.md):

  • How brain tissue scatters each colour (Jacques 2013), and how forward the scattering is (Yona 2016).
  • How blood absorbs each colour (Prahl, OMLC tables).
  • Heat constants of brain and blood (Elwassif 2006).
  • The ChR2 switch models (Williams 2013, and the PyRhO fit).
  • Off-times of Chronos and ChrimsonR (Klapoetke 2014).
  • The Hodgkin-Huxley nerve-cell equations (1952).
  • Heating figures from Stujenske et al. 2015, used only as a comparison.

Toy (our simplifications):

  • The tissue is one uniform block: no layers, no blood vessels, no skull, no fibre body.
  • Only blood absorbs light (3 % blood volume, 75 % oxygen assumed). No other absorption, so red light probably looks better than it is.
  • One scattering set for every colour, with the forward-scattering value from 473 nm.
  • One Williams 2013 rate read as 10-5 instead of the printed 105.
  • Chronos and ChrimsonR are simplified: the ChR2 model with only the closing speed changed. Their real light sensitivity, fading and recovery are not modelled.
  • The nerve cell is a squid-axon model sped up 3 times (16.3 °C instead of 6.3 °C). Every switch gives the same 15 µA/cm² peak current, so only speed differs. One cell, no dendrites, no network.
  • One switch-on level, 3 mW/mm², for every switch. Real levels differ by switch, expression and cell type.
  • The fibre sends light evenly inside its cone. The heat model's edges stay at body temperature about  mm from the tip. The glass fibre's own heat flow is ignored.
  • 20-pulse trains of 2 ms pulses, rates 10 to 200 Hz. "Keeps up" means at least 95 % of pulses give exactly one spike (one miss allowed). A switch's speed is the last rate before its first failure.
  • This model runs about times hotter than a published model of the same kind (Stujenske et al. 2015).
