# Claims ledger: Medicine 2026 (optogenetics)

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research, and not for any lab or clinical use.

Every factual sentence we say in a video or print in a README comes from this table, word for word. If a sentence changes, its row is checked again. The wider background is in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md).

**Check date:** 2026-10-06. All 27 sources below were opened again on that date and the claim was found on the page (details in the last column and in "How the checks were done").

**How verified**

- **committee text**: the Nobel Committee's own press release, summary page, popular-science PDF or scientific-background PDF.
- **primary paper**: the research paper itself (the PubMed abstract, or the full text where it is free).
- **author's account**: a first-person account published in a journal by the person involved.
- **prize page**, **journal editorial**, **company release**: the page of the body that gave the prize, the journal that wrote the editorial, or the company that made the announcement.
- **reputable news**: a news report from an established outlet, used only to back up a company or committee statement.

**Status:** "verified" means the claim sentence is supported by the page as it stood on the check date. A note after it says what to keep in mind.

## How the checks were done

On 2026-10-06 every source above was opened again and the claim sentence was looked for on the page. These are the sources that were re-opened:

- Nobel press release page, summary page, the popular-science PDF and the scientific-background PDF. These were downloaded and their text was searched.
- The full text of Nagel et al. 2003 (PMC283525), Boyden 2011 (PMC3155186) and Deisseroth 2015 (PMC4790845).
- The Nature Methods editorial, the Brain Prize page, the Lasker Foundation page and the Nanoscope release, plus the Ophthalmology Times and R&D World articles.
- The PubMed records for the papers cited in C07, C08, C12, C13, C15, C16, C20, C22, C23 and C24. PubMed's own web page asks for a browser cookie, so the same records were read through the NCBI E-utilities service (the official PubMed interface), which returns the same title, authors, journal, year and abstract.

## Checks that failed or changed a sentence

- **Crick (C15).** The original paper could not be searched (it is a page scan). The claim rests on the committee's quotation. The first draft said Crick "predicted" optogenetics; the sentence now says he "wrote" that light would be the ideal signal.
- **Alga speed (C04).** The Nobel Committee's two documents give different figures for the alga's delay (about half a millisecond in the popular text, "only microseconds" in the scientific text). The ledger uses the popular text.
- **Heating (C22).** An earlier note said light heated neurons "even without opsins" in the Owen et al. study. That wording is not in the abstract, so it is not used. The opsin-free claim rests on Stujenske et al. (C23).
- **Fibre light (C21).** An earlier note said fibres "had to emit" 100 times more light. The source says the interface is expected to need that, because of scattering, so the sentence uses "describes".
- **Approval (C25).** "No optogenetic therapy is approved" is not stated on any single page. The sentence says "it is under review and has not been approved", which follows from the company's "If approved" wording. Re-checked on 6 October 2026, the day this repository went public: still under review, no approval announced.
- **Not re-opened.** The Broad Institute biography page for Feng Zhang refused the request (HTTP 403). The claim that depended on it was replaced by a PubMed record of the 2013 CRISPR paper (in FACTS.md), and nothing in this ledger relies on that page.
