# "Guess first, then see" page (Economics 2026)

> **Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.**

Open the page: <https://faviovazquez.github.io/nobel-2026-lab/2026/economics/page/> (or open `index.html` from a local copy).

One self-contained page about the 2026 prize in economic sciences (Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford, "for
microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation"). It has two parts, like the film. The visitor guesses
first, with a slider or a button, then reveals what the toys say:

Every reveal reads in about ten seconds on a phone: one big number (or the two benchmark lines), one or two plain
sentences and a small chart. Everything else (secondary shares, out of sample, adjusted, p-values, the
weak-instrument details, eigenvalues, the full quotes and the toys' `INSIGHTS.md` sentences) sits under the closed
"More on this answer" of the same reveal. A browser check counts the words a reveal shows before it.

1. **Can you predict spending?** How much of next quarter's change in US consumer spending the past predicts. The
   reveal leads with the committee's framing: Hall's benchmark pictures households who all look ahead, which makes
   the change unpredictable; a predictable part is the trace of households who spend as they earn, which the next
   question measures. Then the toy's headline share (its `guess.a` answer) and the chart; no verdict words. Then a
   second slider: the share of income that goes to households who spend as they earn. Two short benchmark lines
   (Campbell and Mankiw 1989, "about 50 percent" of income; the prize committee on Hall and Mishkin 1982, about
   20 % of consumption, "hand-to-mouth", in their 1969-1975 sample; quotes under 15 words, cut by `build_data.py`
   from the toy's quotes), then our estimate only as a wide range with its weak-instrument warning. The full quotes,
   the first-stage F, the Anderson-Rubin set and the simulated control are under "More". From the
   [can-you-predict-spending toy](../can-you-predict-spending/).
2. **The lab with three equations.** Three guesses as buttons: (a) an energy-cost shock, does the rule raise or lower
   the rate? (b) a rate that rises less than one for one with inflation, what happens in the model? (c) a promise
   about the rate two years out, does it move inflation today? Each reveal draws the toy's impulse responses (output
   gap, inflation, policy rate) as three small SVG charts, every curve labelled "textbook model, illustrative
   calibration", with one plain sentence; (b) draws no path for the passive rule (the model has many) and keeps the
   eigenvalues against the unit circle under "More"; (c) ends with the forward guidance puzzle in one sentence and
   its citation, the ratios under "More". From the [set-the-rate toy](../set-the-rate/).

These are the model's answers and what one test finds in one data set, not advice. No current events, no named
central bank or politician (the prize's official name, "Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of
Alfred Nobel", appears once, in the credits; the titles stay without "Nobel"); the committee's words appear only as
credited quotes.

Every number in the page's text is read from `data.js`; none is typed into `index.html` (a test checks the HTML
text and every string in the script). The narrator sentences are the toys' `INSIGHTS.md` lines, unchanged. If any
input is not `"final"`, a box under the top label says which one ("Preliminary", or "Placeholder numbers" for the
fixtures). If the spending toy has no data licence (synthetic only), a box says part one is simulated and its
numbers are labelled "simulated".

**Field names are read, not typed.** The toys were still being revised while the page was built, so `build_data.py`
finds the headline of each guess as its `answer*` number, the interval as the `ci*` pair with the same stem
(`answer_pct` with `ci95_pct`, `x_pct` with `x_ci95_pct`), and lists every other number of `guess.a` small, with a
label made from its key (`lags_1_4_pct` becomes "lags 1-4"). The impulse responses are found by the variables named
in the toy's `units` block. A field it cannot find is printed as a WARNING (also kept in `data.js` under
`warnings`), and that piece of the page is left out; nothing is filled in.

## Open it

Double-click `index.html`, or from this folder: `open index.html` (macOS) / `xdg-open index.html` (Linux).
No server, no network, no external fonts, scripts or images. It works from `file://` and from GitHub Pages. Light
first, like the film: a warm ledger-paper ground with one ink (green) and one accent (crimson); the dark theme
follows the system setting or the theme button.

## Files

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | the page: HTML, CSS and plain JavaScript in one file; the charts are inline SVG made by the script |
| `data.js` | generated by `build_data.py`; sets `window.NOBEL_ECON_DATA` (about 20 KB) |
| `build_data.py` | reads the two toys' results JSON and INSIGHTS.md and writes `data.js` (Python 3 standard library only) |
| `run_all.py` | one command: `build_data.py`, then the browser checks, screenshots and `og.jpg` |
| `make_fonts.py` | subsets the three OFL font families into `fonts/` (only needed if the page gains new characters) |
| `fonts/` | Fraunces, Inter and JetBrains Mono subsets (WOFF2), renamed "Econ Page ..."; `OFL.txt`, `OFL-1.1.txt`, `faces.txt` |
| `og.jpg` | 1200x630 share image (the page opened at `index.html#og`) |
| `screenshots/` | `page_{360,768,1440}_{light,dark}.png`, the full page after every guess (stored with a 128-colour palette when Pillow is there) |
| `tests/` | `test_build_data.py` (pytest), `test_page.mjs` (headless Chromium) and `test_page.py` (runs it under pytest); `fixtures/` holds copies of the two results files marked `"fixture"` |

## Rebuild

From `2026/economics/` (the toys sit next to the page; `--from` points at results folders anywhere else):

```bash
cd 2026/economics
python3 -m page.run_all                   # ../can-you-predict-spending/results and ../set-the-rate/results
python3 -m page.run_all --from SPEND_RESULTS RATE_RESULTS
python3 -m page.run_all --fixtures        # the copies in page/tests/fixtures (the page says "Placeholder numbers")
python3 -m page.run_all --no-browser      # only rebuild data.js
```

`build_data.py` prints the status of each input and any WARNING. A results file with no `"status"` field counts as
final. To refresh the fixtures from the toys: `python3 page/tests/fixtures/make_fixtures.py SPEND_TOY_DIR RATE_TOY_DIR`.

## Tests

```bash
cd 2026/economics/page
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 -m pytest -q tests
```

The browser check needs Node 18+ and `playwright-core` with a Chromium build. It defaults to showtime's copies
(`~/.showtime/node/node_modules/playwright-core`, `~/.showtime/browsers`); set `PLAYWRIGHT_CORE` and
`PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH` to use others. It opens the page from `file://` at 360, 768 and 1440 px in light and
dark (one run with reduced motion), makes both slider guesses and all three button guesses (one wrong pick first),
and checks: the label comes first, the answers stay hidden until a guess, part one leads with the bridge sentence
before the number, each reveal stays under its word limit before a closed "More on this answer" that holds the
secondary numbers, every number shown is in `data.js`, our share of hand-to-mouth income appears only as a range
with its weak-instrument warning, every curve carries the label "textbook model, illustrative calibration" and has
one point per JSON value, no named central bank (other than the prize's official name, once, in the credits), no
advice words and no verdict words in the page's own text, no "Nobel" in the title, no network requests, no console errors, no sideways
scrolling, the bundled fonts load, the charts have a title and description, and the credits are there. It also
opens the page with the fixtures, with the rate toy missing, and with a synthetic-only spending toy.

## Fonts

Fraunces (headings, big numbers, quotes), Inter (body) and JetBrains Mono (kickers, chart labels), the faces of the
film, all under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, from the `@fontsource-variable` packages. `make_fonts.py --src DIR`
subsets them to the characters the page uses (needs `fonttools` and `brotli`); `fonts/OFL.txt` lists the copyright
lines.
