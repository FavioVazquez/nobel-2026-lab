# Nobel Peace Prize 2026: Navi Pillay fact sheet

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.

This is the sourced fact sheet behind the Peace folder. Every statement names where it comes from. It was compiled on
2026-10-09, the day of the announcement. Primary sources first: nobelprize.org (the press release, summary, facts and
announcement pages), the Nobel Prize API v2.1, the ICTR's own judgments on the IRMCT legacy site, UN documents
(documents.un.org) and the ICJ's reports to the UN General Assembly. Secondary sources only where no primary record
was reachable, and marked as such. Every quote below is at most 15 words. The sentences we actually use in the video,
the page and the READMEs are listed one by one, with a check date, in [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md); the ids in brackets
(P01, P02, ...) point to that ledger.

Scope: this sheet covers only the career rows listed here and the committee lines listed in section 4. No other
sentence of the official pages is used in this folder. The conflict data (UCDP) and our toy results are not facts
about her and live in the experiment folders, each with its own label; their numbers are in CLAIMS.md (H and A rows).

Likeness: one credited photo (U.S. Mission Geneva, 2012, CC BY 2.0) and one credited footage excerpt (ONU Brasil, 2013,
CC BY 3.0) appear in the video only; the Nobel illustration of the laureate is not used anywhere (section 7).

## 5. The ICTR case fork (resolved from the judgments)

### What the committee says
[PR], one paragraph: the ICTR "established that rape and sexual violence could constitute a crime against humanity –
as well as genocide", and "her influence was a significant factor". Then: "In the same case, Ms Pillay was instrumental
in a separate legal breakthrough: for the first time, a defendant faced charges of incitement to commit genocide purely
on the basis of spreading propaganda." The committee names no case.
[FACTS] (nobelprize.org) puts both together without "same case": "sexual violence and rape as well as the spreading of
propaganda could lead to indictment for crimes against humanity and genocide".

### What the court record says

**Prosecutor v. Jean-Paul Akayesu, ICTR-96-4-T. Trial judgment 2 September 1998.** [AKA-TJ]
- Bench (cover page): "Judge Laïty Kama, Presiding / Judge Lennart Aspegren / Judge Navanethem Pillay". She sat as one
  of three judges; she did **not** preside. Same composition in [A/54/315] para 14.
- Accused: bourgmestre (mayor) of Taba commune [AKA-TJ] para 1.
- Rape charges were added mid-trial: "It was amended during the trial, in June 1997, with the addition of three counts"
  (counts 13-15) [AKA-TJ] para 6; amended 17 June 1997 (para 10). The allegations "first came to the attention of the
  Chamber through the testimony of Witness J" (para 416; "On examination by the Chamber" in the same paragraph; the
  judgment does not say which judge asked).
- Rape defined (para 688): "physical invasion of a sexual nature, committed on a person under circumstances which are
  coercive" (the sentence opens "The Tribunal defines rape as a").
- Rape and sexual violence as genocide (para 731): "they constitute genocide in the same way as any other act" (when
  committed with intent to destroy the group); "Sexual violence was an integral part of the process of destruction".
- Verdict (unanimous): guilty on 9 of 15 counts, including "Count 1: Guilty of Genocide", "Count 4: Guilty of Direct
  and Public Incitement to Commit Genocide", "Count 13: Guilty of Crime against Humanity (Rape)". [A/54/315] para 14:
  "found Jean Paul Akayesu guilty of 9 of the 15 counts".
- The incitement count was about **speeches at a public meeting**, not media: Count 4 relates "essentially, to the
  speeches that Akayesu reportedly made at a meeting" in Gishyeshye on 19 April 1994 (para 672); the Chamber found a
  causal link to the massacres in Taba (paras 673-675). The incitement count was in the indictment from the start
  (counts 13-15 were the only additions).
- Sentence (2 Oct 1998) and appeal: several concurrent terms, "a single sentence of life imprisonment"; the Appeals
  Chamber (1 June 2001) "Affirms the verdict of guilty ... and the sentence of life imprisonment" [AKA-AJ] paras 8, 422
  and Disposition. (Several concurrent terms, served as one life sentence: both descriptions found in secondary sources are true.)

**Prosecutor v. Nahimana, Barayagwiza and Ngeze ("Media case"), ICTR-99-52-T. Trial judgment 3 December 2003.** [MED-TJ]
- Bench (cover page, read on the page image): "Navanethem Pillay, presiding / Erik Møse / Asoka de Zoysa Gunawardana".
  She **presided**. The Security Council kept her on the case after her ICTR term ended [SRES1482] para 1(d).
- Accused (judgment paras 5-9): Nahimana, a founder of the radio station RTLM ("charged mainly in relation to the radio
  station called" RTLM); Barayagwiza, a founding member of the CDR party and of the committee that founded RTLM
  (charged mainly over RTLM and the CDR); Ngeze, founder and "Editor-in-Chief" of the newspaper Kangura.
- The Chamber on the case itself (para 945): "have not been addressed at the level of international criminal justice
  since Nuremberg" (of "the role of the media"); "Those who control such media are accountable for its consequences."
- Verdict (unanimous): all three guilty of conspiracy to commit genocide, genocide, direct and public incitement to
  commit genocide, and crimes against humanity (persecution, extermination) (paras 1092-1094). Sentences: Nahimana
  life, Barayagwiza 35 years (reduced for violations of his rights), Ngeze life (paras 1105-1108).
- Appeal, 28 November 2007 [MED-AJ] Disposition (Judges Pocar presiding, Shahabuddeen, Güney, Vaz, Meron): many
  convictions reversed. What stands on incitement: Nahimana, as a superior (Article 6(3)), "but only in respect of
  RTLM broadcasts after 6 April 1994"; Ngeze, for "matters published in his newspaper Kangura in 1994";
  Barayagwiza's incitement convictions were all reversed (his genocide-instigation, extermination and persecution
  convictions stand). Sentences replaced: 30, 32 and 35 years [MED-AJ]; [MED-CASE]: "sentenced to 35 years'
  imprisonment on 28 November 2007" (Ngeze), 32 (Barayagwiza, "Died while serving sentence"), 30 (Nahimana).

**The tribunal's own summary of its "firsts"** [ICTR], written by the ICTR/IRMCT, not by us:
- "For the first time in history, an international tribunal - the ICTR - delivered verdicts" against persons
  responsible for genocide.
- "the first international tribunal to define rape in international criminal law"; "the first institution to
  recognise rape as a means of perpetrating genocide".
- The Media case: "the first international tribunal to hold members of the media responsible for broadcasts"
  (continues "intended to inflame the public to commit acts of genocide").

### Answer
- (a) rape and sexual violence as a crime against humanity and as genocide = **Akayesu (1998)**. She was one of three
  judges; Judge Laïty Kama presided.
- (b) "incitement ... purely on the basis of spreading propaganda": the court record fits the **Media case (2003)**,
  which she presided and which the ICTR itself calls the first to hold media responsible. Akayesu also convicted for
  direct and public incitement, but for his speeches at a public meeting, not for media propaganda. So the record does
  not show one case doing both; the committee's "In the same case" cannot be confirmed from the judgments. Media-case
  precedent: Nuremberg (Streicher, for his newspaper) is discussed in [MED-TJ] itself, so "first ever" in our own voice
  is wrong on any reading.
- How to phrase it (true, and not contradicting the committee): three separate layers, never merged.
  1. The committee, in quotation marks and credited: lines 9 and 10 of section 4.
  2. The court record, flat, in our voice, no "first", no "same case", no "her ruling" (all three judgments were
     unanimous Chamber decisions):
     - "Akayesu, 1998. Three judges: Laïty Kama (presiding), Lennart Aspegren, Navanethem Pillay. The Chamber found
       that rape and sexual violence can be acts of genocide and convicted Akayesu of rape as a crime against humanity,
       of genocide, and of direct and public incitement to commit genocide for speeches at a public meeting."
     - "Media case, 2003. Navanethem Pillay presiding, with Erik Møse and Asoka de Zoysa Gunawardana. The Chamber
       convicted a founder of the radio station RTLM, a founding member of the CDR party, and the editor of the
       newspaper Kangura of incitement to commit genocide, among other crimes. On appeal in 2007 several convictions
       were reversed and the sentences cut to 30, 32 and 35 years."
  3. The tribunal's own "first" lines, credited to the ICTR (the [ICTR] page), if a "first" is wanted on screen.
  The lab never states which case the committee meant.
