# Claims ledger: Peace 2026 (Navi Pillay)

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.

Every factual sentence we say in the video or on the page, or print in a README of this folder, comes from this
table. Wording may shorten a row, never extend it. If a sentence changes, its row is checked again. The wider
background and the source keys in square brackets are in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md).

**Check date:** 2026-10-09. The nobelprize.org pages were read from copies saved that morning; the Nobel API was
pulled live and counted by us; the ICTR judgments were read from the IRMCT PDFs (Media case quotes checked on the page
images, the OCR is noisy); UN documents from documents.un.org. The toy numbers (rows P61 to P84) were checked against the
toys' results JSON on the same day, field by field.

**How verified**

- **Nobel text**: the Norwegian Nobel Committee's press release, the nobelprize.org summary, facts, announcement and
  prize-facts pages, and the list of women laureates.
- **Nobel API**: the Nobel Prize API v2.1 (CC0), counted by us; every count is also stated on nobelprize.org.
- **court record**: the ICTR's judgments (trial and appeal) on the IRMCT legacy site, and the tribunal's own summary
  pages, credited to the tribunal.
- **UN documents**: the ICTR's 1999 annual report, Security Council resolution 1482 (2003), the Secretary-General's
  notes on the High Commissioner's term, and the ICC's and ICJ's reports to the General Assembly.
- **secondary**: South African History Online and Harvard Law School news, only where no primary record was reachable
  and only when the two agree.
- **toy JSON**: a number computed by one of our toys from UCDP data (CC BY 4.0); the row names the JSON field.

**Status:** "verified" means the sentence is supported by a primary source as it stood on the check date (or, where
marked, by two independent secondary sources that agree). "needs second source" means one source only: not used.
"transcribed" means words heard in the footage, taken from a machine transcript and matched on the frames. "rule"
rows set how something may be used. "do not use" rows exist to stop a sentence.

**Quote rule:** committee words are always in quotation marks and credited "Norwegian Nobel Committee" (press release)
or "nobelprize.org" (facts page). At most 15 words per quotation on screen; a longer committee sentence is split into
quoted parts, never paraphrased into our voice. No comment, image or data of ours is attached to P34 or P35. No chart
of ours is captioned with, or placed under, a committee line about conflicts (P38, P39).

### The fork, as the lab says it

The record shows the two holdings in two cases on which she sat: rape and sexual violence as genocide in Akayesu (1998;
she was one of three judges, Kama presided), and media incitement in the Media case (2003; she presided). Akayesu was
also convicted of incitement, for speeches at a public meeting. The lab therefore:
1. quotes the committee (P43, P44) as the committee, on its own card;
2. states the record (P45-P105) on a separate card, in our voice, with no "first" and no "same case";
3. credits any "first" to the ICTR (P57, P58);
4. never says which case the committee meant, never says the committee is wrong, and never says "her ruling" (the
   Chambers decided unanimously).

## What we will not claim

- Any "first" in our own voice: not first judge or jurist to win (Cassin, 1968), not "first ever incitement or
  propaganda case" (Nuremberg is in the Media judgment itself). The committee's and the ICTR's "firsts" appear only in
  quotation marks, credited.
- Which ICTR case the committee meant, or that it erred. We show the committee's words and the court record side by
  side: rape as genocide in Akayesu (1998; she was one of three judges, Kama presided), media incitement in the Media
  case (2003; she presided), and Akayesu's own incitement conviction for speeches at a meeting.
- That she presided over Akayesu, wrote any judgment alone, or personally questioned a witness; the Chambers decided
  unanimously and the judgment names no judge for the questions.
- That the Media-case convictions all stand: the 2007 appeal reversed several and cut the sentences to 30, 32 and
  35 years.
- "Defended Nelson Mandela" as our own statement: it is the committee's line; the Robben Island action needs a second
  source.
- Her ICJ role beyond "judge ad hoc in The Gambia v. Myanmar, chosen by The Gambia, since 2019"; no day, no stage, no
  outcome of the case.
- "Acting" judge, or any "first woman / first Black woman / woman of colour" title in South Africa.
- "Nth South African laureate" (the API records birthplace, not nationality); "first woman born in South Africa" is the
  safe line.
- Any number the committee did not give, attributed to the committee; any comment, image or data of ours placed as a
  judgment on the committee's lines about great powers or sanctioned judges.
- Nomination counts, the Nuremberg-event anecdote, the "could not reach her" detail, the Oslo/10 December logistics,
  ICTR fugitive figures.
- Anything about her career that is not a row in this ledger.
- The Nobel illustration of the laureate (© Nobel Prize Outreach), the Nobel logo or medal, or any implied endorsement
  by Nobel Prize Outreach.
