# Nobel 2026, Physics: a telescope made of ice (about 2:20, 16:9; also an interactive page that stops and asks, and a 60 s vertical cut)

Contract: In about two and a half minutes a curious non-scientist learns why catching high-energy neutrinos from space takes a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice, makes three guesses along the way, watches our toy telescope work out which way a particle was going, sees a real 6 PeV shower, and ends knowing what was discovered (high-energy neutrinos from space) and what is still open (which objects make them).

Rules: every factual sentence is a row of 2026/physics/CLAIMS.md (P-ids in the last column); every toy-model number comes from the experiment results (K = kilometre, T = telescope, filled in after the independent review) and is always "our toy model" (one on-screen label string: "our toy model"); showtime is credited only on the end card. Voice: Kokoro af_heart; years and numbers written as words; "NGC 1068" on screen only. Questions (the interactive page stops and asks; the MP4 holds a 3.5 s pause-and-think beat with the question as a card, a timer ring and the chips): q1 shot 3, q2 shot 6, q3 shot 8. Roadmap bar (bottom 8%, always visible after shot 2): "1 The ghost particle / 2 A telescope of ice / 3 Our toy telescope / 4 What it found". No line or visual before a question gives its answer away. Overlay zones: roadmap bottom 8%, captions just above it, question card and chips centre-low, "our toy model" label top-left, caveat chip top-right. Look: deep navy ice, amber and cyan accents, Fraunces + Inter (the same palette as the 6 PeV replay). Drawings only: no portraits, no Nobel illustrations, no IceCube press images or sky maps, no Sesame Street.
Data decision (owner, 6 Oct): the public IceCube Glashow event data is used, credited "Event data: IceCube Collaboration, DOI 10.21234/gr2021".
