# Claims ledger: Physics 2026 (IceCube)

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.

Every factual sentence we say in a video or print in a README comes from this table, word for word. If a sentence changes, its row is checked again. The wider background is in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md).

**Check date:** 2026-10-06. All 20 sources below were opened on that date and the claim was found on the page (details in the last column and in "How the checks were done").

**How verified**

- **Nobel text**: the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences' press release, the nobelprize.org summary, facts and interview pages, and the Nobel Committee for Physics' popular-science and scientific-background PDFs.
- **Nobel post**: a post by the Nobel Prize's own account on X.
- **primary paper**: the IceCube Collaboration's paper itself, read in its arXiv version (the abstract, or the full text where the row says so). The journal reference is the one arXiv lists.
- **collaboration page**: IceCube's own web page or press release.

**Status:** "verified" means the claim sentence is supported by the page as it stood on the check date. A note after it says what to keep in mind.

No row here is about a toy model. Results from our own code (a sky scan, a photon simulation, the Glashow replay) are not facts; they live in the experiment folders, with their own labels.

## How the checks were done

On 2026-10-06 every source above was opened and the claim sentence was looked for in its text. These are the sources that were opened:

- The Nobel press release, summary, facts and interview pages, the popular-science PDF and the scientific-background PDF. They were downloaded with a standard browser user agent, the PDFs were converted to text, and the text was searched. Page numbers for the scientific background are the document's own ("p. N of 18").
- The two posts by the Nobel Prize on X. They were read through X's public embed service, which returned the post text and the time stamps (09:48:03 and 09:49:26 UTC on 6 October 2026).
- IceCube's detector page and its 10 March 2021 press release on the Glashow event.
- The arXiv abstract pages of the ten IceCube papers in the ledger. The full texts of the Glashow paper (2110.15051), the NGC 1068 paper (2211.09972), the IceCube-170922A paper (1807.08816) and the IceTracks-DR2 paper (2605.19040) were downloaded and searched, because their key numbers are not all in the abstracts.
- The journal versions in Science, Nature and Physical Review Letters were not opened, because most are paywalled. We rely on the arXiv versions, which arXiv links to those journal references.

## Checks that failed or changed a sentence

- **Fingernail (P11).** The brief asked about "a thumbnail". The Nobel text says "your little finger nail", so the sentence says little fingernail.
- **Glashow wording (P35, P36).** IceCube's 2021 headline says the detection "proves 60-year-old theory". The paper says "consistent with", and the significance against ordinary collisions is 2.3σ for one event. The sentences follow the paper.
- **Glashow date (P33).** IceCube's release first gave the date as 6 December 2016. A correction note dated 16 March 2021 changed it to 8 December 2016, which matches the paper.
- **Glashow energy (P33, P34).** 6.05 ± 0.72 PeV is the measured shower energy. 6.3 PeV is the resonance peak, and the paper infers about 6.3 PeV for the neutrino once the energy of shower particles that do not shine is added back. The ledger keeps the two numbers apart.
- **The Glashow event is not in the Nobel documents.** None of the six Nobel texts mentions it. P33 to P38 rest on IceCube's paper and release only.
- **2013: evidence, not discovery (P19).** The scientific background (p.11) calls the 2013 result the "discovery of 28" neutrinos. The paper and the popular text say "evidence". The sentence uses "evidence"; "discovery" is kept for the diffuse flux as a whole (P20).
- **Completion year (P14).** IceCube says the last string went in on 18 December 2010; the Nobel texts say 2011. The sentence gives both.
- **The interview (P07, P08).** Research notes written earlier on the same day said the Nobel interview page returned "404". It was online when checked, with a full transcript, and both quotes come from it.
- **Preprints (P29, P30, P32).** The 14-year data-release paper and the 12-year Milky Way paper are on arXiv with no journal reference on the check date. The sentences say "preprint" or name the paper, and P32 also rests on the committee's own wording.
- **Left out.** Halzen's age (no source states it); his named professorship (sources differ); the exact angle between IceCube-170922A and TXS 0506+056 (the paper and the committee give different figures); and any number produced by our own code.
