# "Guess first, then see" page (Physics 2026)

> **Educational demo made to show an open-source tool. Toy model, not research.**

Open the page: <https://faviovazquez.github.io/nobel-2026-lab/2026/physics/page/> (or open `index.html` from a local copy).

One self-contained page about the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics (Francis Halzen, IceCube). A visitor makes a
guess, then reveals what our toy models say, with the caveat right next to every number:

1. **The odds.** What is the chance that a 100 TeV neutrino interacts while crossing 1 km of ice? Choices are
   orders of magnitude (1 in 10 up to 1 in 10 million). The answer and the curve from 1 TeV to 10 PeV come
   from the [kilometre experiment](../kilometre/).
2. **How big a detector?** Cosmic neutrinos per year in a 1 km cube of ice, then a size slider from 10 m to
   2 km with 10 m, 100 m and 1 km marked. Then **the Earth as a shield**: how many 1 PeV neutrinos get
   straight up through the Earth, with an angle slider and a drawing of the path. Same experiment.
3. **Build a telescope.** At 250 m between strings instead of IceCube's 125 m, how much worse is the aim?
   Then a spacing slider (50 to 300 m) over the median angular error, IceCube's 125 m marked, the real
   IceCube layout as its own point, and one typical simulated event drawn from above or from the side (hits
   coloured by time, true and fitted directions, plus a zoom on the sky). From the
   [telescope experiment](../telescope/). Caveat: "tuned constants, trend only, not IceCube's performance".
4. **A real event.** A link card to the 3D replay of IceCube's 6 PeV event, [`../glashow/`](../glashow/).

Every number in the page's text is read from `data.js`; none is typed into `index.html`. If any input is not
`"final"`, a red "Preliminary" box under the top label says which one, and each number from it carries a
"preliminary" (or "placeholder") tag.

## Open it

Double-click `index.html`, or from this folder: `open index.html` (macOS) / `xdg-open index.html` (Linux).
No server, no network, no external fonts, scripts or images (the step 4 card shows `../glashow/og.jpg` from this
repository). It works from `file://` and from GitHub Pages. Light and dark themes follow the system setting.

## Files

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | the page: HTML, CSS and plain JavaScript in one file; charts and drawings are inline SVG made by the script |
| `data.js` | generated by `build_data.py`; sets `window.NOBEL_PHYS_DATA` (about 8 KB) |
| `build_data.py` | reads the two experiments' results and writes `data.js` (Python 3 standard library only) |
| `run_all.py` | one command: `build_data.py`, then the browser checks, screenshots and `og.jpg` |
| `og.jpg` | the 1200 x 630 share image, a screenshot of `index.html#og` |
| `screenshots/` | the page after every reveal, at 360, 768 and 1440 px, light and dark |
| `tests/` | `test_build_data.py` (pytest), `test_page.mjs` (headless Chromium), `test_page.py` (runs it under pytest), `fixtures/` |

## Where the numbers come from

`build_data.py` reads, relative to this folder:

| input | used for |
|---|---|
| `../kilometre/results/kilometre_summary.json` | `status`; `p_interact_1km` and `one_in_N_100TeV` (step 1); `events_per_year` and `hese_check` (step 2); `earth_survival_vertical` and `details.earth_survival_vertical_per_species` (Earth shield) |
| `../kilometre/results/interaction_chance_1km.csv` | step 1 curve: column `p_mean` (neutrino and antineutrino averaged, the same definition as the headline numbers) |
| `../kilometre/results/events_vs_size.csv` | step 2 curve, 10 m to 2 km |
| `../kilometre/results/earth_transmission_numu.csv` | Earth-shield curve: the 1 PeV row, one column per zenith angle (muon neutrinos only; the page says so, because the headline vertical number averages neutrinos and antineutrinos) |
| `../telescope/results/telescope_summary.json` | `status`; medians, the 16-84 % band, hits, the share of muons that could be fitted, the real-layout point, the muon energy (step 3) |
| `../telescope/results/example_event.json` + `../telescope/data/icecube86_geometry.csv` | the drawn event: sensor index to position (row order of the geometry file, as `example_event.json` states), first-photon time, charge, true and fitted directions and points |

The curve files are found by their column names (energy and probability; zenith and transmission; cube side
and events per year), so a renamed file still works; `tests/test_build_data.py` covers the layouts it accepts.
Run it again after either experiment is re-run. It prints the status of each input and what it found:

```bash
python3 build_data.py                     # from 2026/physics/page/ after the merge (../kilometre, ../telescope)
python3 build_data.py --from /path/to/kilometre/results /path/to/telescope/results   # results folders anywhere
python3 build_data.py --fixtures          # placeholder inputs in tests/fixtures (status "fixture")
```

`tests/fixtures/` holds placeholder inputs that follow the two experiments' schemas, so the page can be
built and tested without them. They are rough hand estimates, not results, and the page labels them
"placeholder" (`tests/fixtures/make_fixtures.py` writes them; it needs NumPy and the nuFATE tables).

## What the page itself computes

Only presentation, no physics:

- **Sliders between computed points.** Step 2's size slider reads the experiment's curve, straight lines
  between its points on log-log axes (the toy rate grows as the volume, so this is exact for the toy). The
  Earth angle slider uses straight lines in log(transmission) between the curve's 11 angles. The spacing
  slider uses straight lines between the 7 sweep points and says "interpolated" between them.
- **Right, close or not quite.** A guess is right if it is the choice nearest to the toy number on a log
  scale, "close" if it is the neighbouring choice.
- **The step 3 ratio** is the Pandel-fit median at 250 m divided by the one at 125 m.
- **The path length** in the Earth readout is 2 x 6,371 km x sin(angle below the horizon) (Earth radius as in
  the nuFATE density model); the core in the drawing is at 3,480 km (the same model's core boundary).
  The drawing is a sketch, not to scale for the detector.
- **The sky zoom** in step 3 projects each fitted direction onto a plane square to the true direction (degrees).

Fixed text on the page, with sources: 5,160 sensors and 125 m between strings (IceCube detector page and
JINST 12 P03012, arXiv:1612.05093); the prize citation (nobelprize.org, 2026 summary); the 6 PeV event, 8
December 2016 and its 18,135 pulses (the [glashow replay's README](../glashow/README.md) and its sources).

## Test it

Needs Node 18+ and `playwright-core` with a Chromium build (showtime installs both under `~/.showtime`), and
Python 3 with pytest; Pillow is optional (it shrinks the screenshots). See `requirements.txt`.

```bash
python3 -m pytest -q tests                  # build_data checks + the browser check (about 30 s)
PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH=~/.showtime/browsers node tests/test_page.mjs --shots --og   # also screenshots and og.jpg
cd .. && python3 -m page.run_all            # rebuild data.js, check, screenshots and og.jpg in one go
```

The browser check opens `index.html` from `file://` at 360, 768 and 1440 px in light and dark themes, makes
every guess (step 1 by keyboard only), reveals every answer, moves every slider and checks: the reveal waits
for a guess; each answer, its "trend only" label and its caveat appear; step 3 says "tuned constants, trend
only, not IceCube's performance" and marks 125 m; the stated numbers match `data.js`; the side view toggles
and every hit is drawn; every chart has a title and a description; no sideways scrolling; no console
errors; no network requests; page + data under 1.5 MB; share tags present and no canonical link. It also
checks that a copy with every input final hides the "Preliminary" box and that a copy without the
kilometre results says so and still has no errors.
