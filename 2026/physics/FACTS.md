# Nobel Prize in Physics 2026: IceCube fact sheet

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.

This is the sourced fact sheet behind the Physics folder. Every statement names where it comes from. It was compiled on 2026-10-06, the day of the announcement, from the official Nobel documents (the press release, the summary and facts pages, the popular-science PDF, the 18-page scientific background PDF and the laureate's first telephone interview), from the Nobel Prize's own posts on X, from the IceCube Collaboration's papers (read in their arXiv versions) and from IceCube's own web pages. The claims we actually use in videos and READMEs are listed one by one, with a check date, in [CLAIMS.md](CLAIMS.md). The ids in brackets below (P01, P02, ...) point to that ledger.

Toy-model results are not facts and do not belong in this file or in the ledger. They live in the experiment folders, each with its own label.

## 1. Citation and date

- **Citation:** "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin" ([PR], [SUM], [FACTS], [X1]) (P01)
- **Announced:** 6 October 2026 by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences ([PR]). The Nobel Prize's X post with the citation is time-stamped 09:48 UTC that day ([X1]).
- **Share:** 1/1. Halzen is the only laureate ([SUM], [FACTS]). The prize is 12 million Swedish kronor ([PR]). (P02)
- **Press-release headline:** "Elusive particles from the universe captured at the South Pole" ([PR])
- **Committee chair quote:** Halzen "has led an international team of researchers and engineers who have provided us with a fantastic instrument" (Mark Pearce, Chair of the Nobel Committee for Physics, [PR]) (P06)
- **What the prize recognises, in the committee's words:** "A diffuse flux of astrophysical neutrinos extending to petaelectronvolt energies (10^15 eV) has been discovered using data from the IceCube Neutrino Observatory" ([SCI] p.15). This is the flux as a whole. The committee does not claim that individual sources are settled ([SCI] p.16, "Time will tell if these observations confirm AGNs as sources").
- **Illustrations:** by Johan Jarnestad, "free to use for non-commercial purposes" with attribution ([PR]). We do not reuse them.

## 2. The laureate

| | Francis Halzen |
|---|---|
| Born | 23 March 1944, Tienen, Belgium ([FACTS]) (P03) |
| Affiliation at the time of the award | University of Wisconsin–Madison, USA ([FACTS], [PR]) (P03) |
| Training | PhD 1969, KU Leuven, Belgium ([PR]) |
| Role in IceCube | "Principal Investigator from its beginnings" and "the driving force" behind IceCube ([SCI] p.2); Principal Investigator on the 1999 proposal to the US National Science Foundation ([SCI] p.8) (P04) |
| Where the news reached him | A hotel room in Bergamo, Italy, after a science festival, while writing a proposal ([INT]) |

**What the committee credits to him:**

- **The idea.** He and John G. Learned first reported a detector that uses ice as both target and light source in June 1988 ([SCI] p.7). They first presented it at a conference in Poland in 1988 ([POP] p.2). The press release credits Halzen with first presenting "his vision" in 1988 ([PR]). (P05)
- **Learned's part.** Learned was then spokesperson of DUMAND, an ocean detector off Hawaii. He stayed with DUMAND, while Halzen devoted his career to detection in ice ([SCI] p.7; [POP] pp.2-3).
- **Leadership.** He was a driving force on AMANDA, IceCube's forerunner, built in phases between 1993 and 2000 ([SCI] p.7). As Principal Investigator he carried IceCube "from its invention and conceptual design, through the technically audacious construction, to routine science operations" ([SCI] p.15).
- **The team.** IceCube is run by about 450 people from 58 institutions in 14 countries ([SCI] p.2). (P18)

## 3. The story in 15 lines

1. On 6 October 2026 the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences gave the whole Nobel Prize in Physics to Francis Halzen, of the University of Wisconsin–Madison. (P01, P02, P03)
2. Neutrinos have no electric charge and almost no mass, and they seldom hit anything. They pass through the Earth and through our bodies without us noticing. (P09, P10)
3. Every second, 65 billion neutrinos from the Sun pass through your little fingernail. (P11)
4. Neutrinos are not bent by magnetic fields, so their direction points back toward where they were made. That makes them messengers from cosmic particle accelerators. (P12)
5. Halzen first presented the idea of catching neutrinos in the South Pole ice in 1988, with John G. Learned. (P05)
6. High-energy cosmic neutrinos are very rare, so an enormous volume of ice is needed to catch enough of them. Of about 100,000 neutrinos IceCube registers each year, only about 100 are expected to come from space. (P16, P17)
7. IceCube is a cubic kilometre of ice watched by 5,160 light sensors on 86 strings, between 1,450 and 2,450 metres deep. (P13)
8. The last string went into the ice on 18 December 2010. The Nobel texts say the detector was finished, or reached its full size, in 2011. (P14)
9. IceCube does not see neutrinos directly. It sees the blue Cherenkov light from charged particles that a neutrino makes when it hits the ice. (P15)
10. Halzen was Principal Investigator from the beginning and led a collaboration that today has about 450 people. He calls the clear deep ice "pure luck", and says his main reaction over the years "was not proud, but was surprised". (P04, P18, P07, P08)
11. In 2013 IceCube reported 28 high-energy events and the first evidence (4 sigma) for neutrinos from space. With a third year of data the purely atmospheric explanation was rejected at 5.7 sigma. (P19, P20)
12. In 2017 a single neutrino of about 290 TeV, IceCube-170922A, arrived from the direction of a flaring blazar, TXS 0506+056. The papers call the link evidence (3 sigma, and 3.5 sigma for an earlier flare). (P21, P22)
13. In 2022 IceCube reported 79 neutrinos from the active galaxy NGC 1068 at 4.2 sigma, which the paper calls evidence. The Nobel text says it is not yet robust enough to call NGC 1068 a source. (P23, P24, P25)
14. In 2023 IceCube saw neutrinos from the plane of our own galaxy at 4.5 sigma. A 2026 preprint reaches 5.7 sigma, and the committee calls the Milky Way the first neutrino source above the 5 sigma threshold. (P31, P32)
15. On 8 December 2016 IceCube recorded a 6.05 PeV particle shower that is consistent with the Glashow resonance, proposed in 1960. It is one event, at 2.3 sigma. (P33, P34, P35, P36)

## 5. What we will not say

- **"IceCube discovered the source NGC 1068"**, or "found", "detected" or "identified" it as a source. The paper says "evidence" (4.2 sigma). The Nobel popular text says it is "not robust enough to definitively identify NGC 1068 as a neutrino source" ([POP] p.6). Halzen in his interview calls it "the first source we really identified by ourselves" ([INT]); we do not repeat that phrasing as fact.
- **"Discovery", "proof" or "proves" for the Glashow event.** The paper says "consistent with" and gives 2.3 sigma for a single event. IceCube's own 2021 headline uses "proves 60-year-old theory" ([IC-GR]); we do not use it. Glashow himself is quoted there saying "To be absolutely sure, we should see another such event".
- **That the Glashow event is part of the prize.** None of the Nobel documents mentions it. It is an IceCube result, sourced to IceCube's paper and release only.
- **"Discovered" for TXS 0506+056.** The papers say evidence (3 sigma for the 2017 coincidence, 3.5 sigma for the 2014-2015 flare).
- **"5 sigma" for any single point source.** The only neutrino source the committee puts above 5 sigma is the Milky Way as a whole, and that rests on a 2026 preprint ([SCI] p.12).
- **"Discovery" for the 2013 result alone.** The 2013 paper says "evidence" at 4 sigma, and so does the popular text. We keep "discovery" for the diffuse flux as a whole (5.7 sigma in 2014, and the committee's own wording).
- **"Halzen built IceCube alone."** He proposed the idea with Learned, was Principal Investigator and led a collaboration of about 450 people.
- **"A thumbnail" or "your thumb".** The Nobel text says "your little finger nail".
- **That IceCube "sees" neutrinos.** It records light from the charged particles a neutrino makes.
- **That any toy model reproduces IceCube**, its significances, its event energies or its sky maps. Our pages and scripts are educational sketches. The Glashow replay shows IceCube's real public data, drawn by us; it is not an official IceCube visualisation, and it measures nothing.
- **Halzen's age.** No source we checked states it, so we leave it out.

## 6. Where the sources disagree

- **Completion year.** IceCube says the last string went in on 18 December 2010 and the detector was "completed in December 2010" ([IC]). The press release says it "was finished in 2011" ([PR]); the popular text says it "reached its full size in 2011" ([POP] p.4); IceCube's own instrumentation paper says construction "was completed in 2011" (<https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05093>). We give both facts (P14).
- **2013: evidence or discovery?** The 2013 paper's title and abstract say "Evidence" and "4 sigma". The scientific background calls it the "discovery of 28 high-energy ... astrophysical neutrinos" ([SCI] p.11). We follow the paper.
- **Sensor count.** 5,160 ([IC], [POP] p.4) versus "over 5000" ([SCI] p.8). Same thing, rounded.
- **Colour of the light.** "A blue light" ([POP] p.2; Halzen in [INT]) versus "UV-dominated" ([SCI] p.5). We say "blue light".
- **Glashow energy.** The shower energy is 6.05 ± 0.72 PeV; the neutrino energy is inferred to be about 6.3 PeV, once the energy of shower particles that do not shine is added back (Nature paper). IceCube's release says the antineutrino carried 6.3 PeV ([IC-GR]). We say "a 6 PeV shower".
- **Glashow date.** IceCube's release first said 6 December 2016 and corrected it to 8 December on 16 March 2021 ([IC-GR], correction note). The paper gives 2016 December 8, 01:47:59 UTC.
- **NGC 1068 count.** The paper gives 79 (+22/−20) neutrinos; the scientific background says 79 neutrinos "in the energy range 1–10 TeV" ([SCI] p.16).
- **Ice clarity.** Light "could travel 300 metres before being absorbed" below 1,400 m ([POP] p.4) versus an absorption length of about 200 m below 2,100 m ([SCI] p.8). We say "hundreds of metres", as Halzen does ([INT]).
- **Spelling.** The popular text spells DUMAND as "DUMAN" ([POP] p.6).
