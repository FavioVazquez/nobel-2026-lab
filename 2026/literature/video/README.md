# Literature 2026: new forms (video, 2:29)

> **Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.** Made with [showtime](https://github.com/FavioVazquez/showtime), an open-source video studio for coding agents.

A two-and-a-half-minute explainer of this year's Nobel Prize in Literature, drawn in ink and wash on warm paper. It says who won (the Canadian author Anne Carson), gives the Swedish Academy's citation phrase by phrase, and shows why "the classical tradition" reaches her in pieces: of more than 120 plays by Sophocles, seven survive; of an estimated 9,000 to 10,000 lines by Sappho, about 650. It shows how editors print a torn papyrus, with brackets and a guess inside them, and one Sappho line carried through 2,000 years of translators. Then, one drawing per book, it shows the new forms the Nobel Committee describes: *Eros the Bittersweet*, voices paired across centuries, *The Glass Essay*, *Autobiography of Red*, *The Beauty of the Husband*, *Nox*, *Float* and the libretti. It ends on the 19th woman and the second Canadian laureate. It asks the viewer no questions: it simply tells the story. The four experiments behind it are the [survival ledger](../survival-ledger/README.md), [fragments](../fragments/README.md), [translators](../translators/README.md) and [forms](../forms/README.md), and you can try them on [the page](https://faviovazquez.github.io/nobel-2026-lab/2026/literature/page/).

## How it was made

With [showtime](https://github.com/FavioVazquez/showtime): the scenes are HTML and canvas drawings that showtime renders frame by frame on one computer, with a local voice, music, captions and checks. The look is a hand-drawn one (ink lines and washes on cold-press paper, strokes that draw themselves), made by our own drawing code. The voice is synthetic (Kokoro, voice am_eric), made locally. Every drawing is ours: there is no photo, no portrait, no silhouette and no cover art of the laureate or her books, and no Nobel logo or medal.

## Her words in the film

Anne Carson is a living author, so the film shows only four short quotations, each from a different work, each in quotation marks and attributed ("Anne Carson, *Work* (year)"), each next to what it comments on. They are not spoken, and they are never shown as being written by us. The wording of each is checked against pages that reproduce it: see the ledger's [Quotations](../CLAIMS.md#quotations-short-quotations-for-commentary) block.

- **Q1**, *Eros the Bittersweet* (1986): the one line the Nobel Committee itself quotes, attributed "as quoted by the Nobel Committee", shown once (L63, rule L09).
- **Q2**, *If Not, Winter* (2002), her introduction: on the brackets that stand for the holes in a torn papyrus (L60), beside the sentence about her Sappho (L73).
- **Q3**, "The Glass Essay" (1995): a line beside the spoken sentence about the poem, which rests on the Committee's words (L64, L68).
- **Q4**, *Autobiography of Red* (1998): her opening line of the retold myth, beside the spoken sentence that the book starts from fragments about Geryon, which rests on the Committee's words (L65, L69).

Nothing else of hers is shown: no passage, no page scan, no cover. The page and the READMEs of this folder keep to their own, shorter list ([quotes.json](../quotes.json)).

## Honest notes

- **Sophocles:** "more than 120" is a modern estimate with no upper limit (the film also shows the Suda's 123, which is ancient testimony). Seven plays survive complete (L27).
- **Sappho:** 9,000 to 10,000 lines is an estimate (Grenfell and Hunt 1914: "something like 9,000"; Green 2015: "some 10,000"); about 650 survive. The dots are only a count: where a dot sits means nothing, and there is no single percentage (L32, L40).
- **The papyrus** is Sappho 16 (Voigt) as Grenfell and Hunt printed it in 1914, typed by hand from the page images. What is inside the brackets is the editors' restoration, a guess; modern editions read some lines differently (L47, L48).
- **The translators:** six versions of Sappho 31, all public domain and all as printed in Wharton's 1908 book. The film says what they open with and ranks none; the lab's word-by-word counts are not shown (L55 to L57).
- **The word for Eros:** the Committee says *Eros the Bittersweet* draws on "a compound adjective from Sappho" and does not name it. The film shows Sappho's word and Wharton's "bitter-sweet" next to that sentence and never says the Committee names it (L58).
- **22 chapbooks:** 22! is pure arithmetic on "twenty-two chapbooks, capable of being read in any order". It says nothing about how the book is meant to be read (L25).
- **Nox:** the film says only what the Committee says (a box, an accordion fold-out, in memory of her brother). The lid is drawn blank and the fold-out is only grey washes and marks: nothing of the book's own pictures or letters, and no name (L14, L71).
- **Autobiography of Red:** red appears only as flat colour; no figure from the book is drawn, and the film says nothing of the story beyond the Committee's "tender hero" sentence (L69).
- **Firsts:** the 19th woman since 1901 and the second Canadian after Alice Munro (2013), never "third" (L18, L19, L43).
- **What the film leaves out on purpose:** the number of languages she is translated into, her university and residence, anything of her private life beyond the Committee's words about *Nox*, and any likeness of her (L41, L42, L46).
