The 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature was awarded to Anne Carson

a classicist who writes poems: so the old books come first

Each part below asks you to guess first, then shows the answer with its source beside it. Every ancient Greek or Latin line and every old English version on this page is public domain, with the edition it comes from. Her own words appear only in a few short quotations, each attributed and set beside what it comments on. There is no picture of her: the drawings are of shelves, papyrus and ink.

I.

How much survives

The classical tradition the citation names is mostly lost. Of the Greek poets and playwrights she translates, what we can read came through a few copies of copies, and most of each author's work did not make it.

II.

Where the words went

Sappho's poems reached us broken in three ways: on torn papyrus dug up in Egypt, inside other writers' quotations that stop where their point was made, and as a word or two quoted by ancient grammarians.

III.

One poem, 2,000 years of translators

IV.

New forms

The citation ends on “new forms for contemporary literature”. Her books, as the Nobel Committee lists them, mix forms that are usually kept apart: essay and verse, novel and poem, translation and stage, the book as an object.
