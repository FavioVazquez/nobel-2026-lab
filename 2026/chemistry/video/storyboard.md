# One hand wins: the Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2026, the full story (about 2:15, 16:9)

Contract: In about two minutes a curious non-chemist learns that many molecules come in mirror-image "hands" and life uses only one; that a reaction left to itself gives 50:50; that in 1953 Charles Frank wrote a three-part recipe for one hand to take over; that Kagan (1986) ticked the second box (a mixed-hand catalyst barely works, so 75:25 drives the reaction 90:10: the product comes out purer than a straight line predicts); that Soai ticked the third (a molecule that copies its own hand: five parts in ten million -> past 99.5% in three runs, and from nothing a hand chosen at random, 19 vs 18); that our own toy "mirror race" shows why (copying alone = a flat spread; add mutual antagonism = one hand wins, a coin toss which); that this is not the origin of life and the mechanism is still debated; and that choosing the hand matters for medicines. Then: the lab is open; made with showtime.
