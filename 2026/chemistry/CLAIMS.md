# Claims ledger: Chemistry 2026 (Kagan and Soai)

> Educational demos made to show an open-source tool. Not research.

Every factual sentence we say in a video or print in a README comes from this table, word for word. If a sentence changes, its row is checked again. The wider background is in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md).

**Check date:** 2026-10-07. All 17 sources below were opened on that date and the claim was found in their text (details in the last column and in "How the checks were done").

**How verified**

- **Nobel text**: the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences' press release, the nobelprize.org summary and facts pages, and the Nobel Committee for Chemistry's popular-science and scientific-background PDFs.
- **primary paper**: the paper itself, read as its abstract on the publisher's page or on PubMed, or as its title and journal record on Crossref. The row says which.
- **laureate's review**: a review or lecture by Kenso Soai himself, used for numbers that the abstracts do not give.
- **arithmetic**: a number we worked out from a sourced number and the definition of ee. The steps are in the row.

**Status:** "verified" means the claim sentence is supported by the source as it stood on the check date. A note after it says what to keep in mind. "conflict" means the sources disagree; the row says which wording we use. "will not say" means the row exists to stop a sentence, not to support one.

No row here is about a toy model. Results from our own code are not facts; they live in the experiment folders, with their own labels. No row describes a lab procedure, an amount, a condition or how to handle a chemical.

## Checks that failed or changed a sentence

- **1990 came first (C18, C19).** The brief described 1995 as the first asymmetric autocatalysis where the product copies its own hand. The scientific background says Soai's 1990 reaction already did that, with the same hand, but lost purity (86% to 35% ee). 1995 was the first with amplification. The sentences say so.
- **The 1995 series (C20).** Three versions exist: Soai's 2 -> 10 -> 57 -> 81 -> 88%, the scientific background's 5 -> 55 -> 87 -> 88 -> 90 -> 90%, and the popular text's 2% -> 87%. All three are for the same unsubstituted alcohol. No 1995 series goes on screen.
- **The 630,000 (C23).** Soai counts the amount of the winning hand; the scientific background calls it the ratio of the two hands. Our arithmetic shows the ratio cannot have grown 630,000 times. The sentence follows Soai.
- **The 80% ee (C15).** The popular figure gives 75:25 and 90:10 but never prints "80% ee" or "50%". Those two numbers are our arithmetic, and the row says so.
- **The reservoir (C16).** The scientific background dates the reservoir effect to Kagan's 1994 follow-up, not the 1986 paper. The sentence carries no year.
- **The head start (C22).** The popular figure 6 places the 0.00005% runs next to the words "starts with a non-chiral solution". The scientific background and Soai's review say those runs started from an added catalyst. The sentence follows them, and the chance-driven runs are a separate row (C24).
- **Thalidomide (C34, C35).** Our research notes relied on Wikipedia for the point that the two forms interconvert in the body. It is now sourced to a 1995 study in people.
- **"Proof of concept" (C29).** The phrase is in the scientific background word for word, with its limit ("not relevant ... in aqueous systems") in the same sentence. We always quote both halves.
- **Mechanism names (C31).** The scientific background names Blackmond, Brown, Denmark and Trapp. Houk appears only as a co-author in its reference list.
