# "Guess first, then see" page (Chemistry 2026)

> **Educational demo made to show an open-source tool. Toy model, not research.**

Open the page: <https://faviovazquez.github.io/nobel-2026-lab/2026/chemistry/page/> (or open `index.html` from a local copy).

One self-contained page about the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai, "for the discovery
of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis"). It looks like a page from a lab notebook:
paper, faint graph lines, hand-inked strokes, and a night version of the same notebook in dark mode. A visitor makes a
guess, then reveals the answer, with the caveat right next to every number. Some answers are real measurements (and
say so); the rest come from toy models (and say "our toy model, trend only").

0. **Your hands are mirror images.** Alanine and its mirror image in 3D, ball and stick, drawn on a canvas. Drag to
   turn both (they stay mirror images). "Try to stack them" slides the mirror molecule onto ours with a proper turn
   that lines up two arms (the N group and the acid group); the other two (the CH<sub>3</sub> group and the H) are
   ringed, because they point the wrong way. No guess here.
1. **Kagan's bend.** "A catalyst that is 75% one hand and 25% the mirror hand: how big is the product's lead of one hand?" Choices
   25 / 50 / 80 / 100% excess. The answer, 80% (90 to 10), comes with our own three-pie drawing (75:25 pieces
   -> 56/38/6 catalysts -> 90:10 working catalysts), then three sliders (catalyst share of one hand, K, g) that drive the live
   curve (line, bulge or sag) from the experiment's own `kagan.js`, and a bar of which catalysts form. Caveat: "the
   textbook ML2 model; real systems vary". From [kagan-curve](../kagan-curve/).
2. **A molecule that copies its own hand.** "Start with a head start of five parts in ten million. After three runs,
   how much of one hand?" The answer is Soai's real 2003 series (0.00005% -> 57% -> 99% -> >99.5%) as a staircase plus
   a log-scale inset, with the toy model's rounds next to it as dashed bars. The toy's two numbers are fitted to runs
   1 and 2; the page says so, and that run 3 is the toy's own answer. Caveat: "one published toy model of several;
   the real mechanism is still debated". From [soai-amplifier](../soai-amplifier/).
3. **The mirror race.** "Start perfectly balanced. Run it 1,000 times. What do you get?" (always 50:50 / one hand
   always wins / a random hand wins each time). Then a live race in the browser (2,000 molecules, exact stochastic
   simulation, "Run again", "Mutual antagonism: on/off"), eight precomputed runs from the experiment, the 10,000-run
   histograms animating from flat (copying only; flat only for our chosen rates) to two spikes (with antagonism), a "stopped early" view and a "watch
   400 runs land" view, and the "is the coin fair?" panel: the toy's split, Soai's 37 real runs (19 to 18, excess
   15-91%, none all one hand) and Singleton and Vo's 54 (27 to 27). Caveat: "toy network, not the Soai mechanism, and not the
   origin of life", with the Nobel Committee's sentence. From [mirror-race](../mirror-race/).
4. **Why it matters.** Medicines (the Nobel popular information's wording only), the origin-of-life caveat, and cards
   linking the three experiment folders and the video folder on GitHub.

Every number in the page's text is read from `data.js`; none is typed into `index.html`. If any input is not
`"final"`, a red "Preliminary" box under the top label says which one, and each toy number from it carries a
"preliminary" (or "placeholder") tag.

## Open it

Double-click `index.html`, or from this folder: `open index.html` (macOS) / `xdg-open index.html` (Linux).
No server, no network, no external fonts, scripts or images. It works from `file://` and from GitHub Pages.
Light and dark themes follow the system setting. Fonts are the system's own (a serif for text, a handwriting face
such as Noteworthy or Bradley Hand for margin notes where the system has one); nothing is downloaded. Animations
jump to their end when the system asks for reduced motion.

Colours: one hand is burnt orange `#c4622d`, always solid; the mirror hand is teal `#2b8a8f`, always hatched (or a
ring, or dashed), so the two differ by pattern as well as colour; idle mixed pairs are grey.

The two experiments' "real" blocks are cross-checked against these and a warning is printed if they differ.
Run it again after any experiment is re-run. It prints the status of each input file:

```bash
python3 build_data.py                     # from 2026/chemistry/page/ after the merge
python3 build_data.py --from MIRROR_RESULTS KAGAN_DIR AMP_RESULTS   # the experiments' folders anywhere
python3 build_data.py --fixtures          # placeholder inputs in tests/fixtures (status "fixture")
```

`tests/fixtures/` holds placeholder inputs in the experiments' layouts, so the page can be built and tested
without them. They are rough stand-ins, not results, and the page labels them "placeholder"
(`tests/fixtures/make_fixtures.py` writes them; it needs NumPy).

## What the page itself computes

Only presentation and one small live toy:

- **The live race** (step 3) is the mirror-race experiment's network, run as an exact stochastic simulation (the
  Gillespie jump chain: pick the next reaction with probability proportional to its rate; waiting times are skipped
  because only the order matters). Plain molecule A turns into one hand or the mirror hand at rate k0 times A each;
  each hand copies itself at rate k1 times A times its count; one hand plus the mirror hand become an idle mixed pair at
  rate k2 times both counts. The rates come from `mirror_summary.json` (k0 = k1 = 1, k2 = 100, dimensionless). The page
  uses 2,000 molecules (a choice: fast on a phone), not the experiment's 10,000. The excess uses the experiment's
  definition: (one hand minus mirror hand) over all handed product, idle pairs counting one of each hand. It uses the
  browser's random numbers, so every run differs.
- **Kagan's curve** calls `eeProd(eeL, K, g)` and `complexes(eeL, K)` from `kagan.js`. The K slider runs from 0.1 to
  1,000 on a log scale and snaps to exactly 4 near it; at 75%, K = 4, g = 0 it reads 80%. If `kagan.js` is missing
  the page falls back to the same closed form (the test checks that the page uses `kagan.js`).
- **The stacking** turns the mirror molecule with a proper rotation (no reflection) that lines up its N and acid-carbon
  directions with ours; the readout gives how far the other two arms then miss (about 2.5 and 1.8 Å).
- **The coin panel's p-values** are exact two-sided binomial tests against 1/2 (`build_data.binom_two_sided`).
- **Right or not quite**: a guess is right only if it is the answer's choice.

## Simplifications (what this page does not claim)

- The toy models are toy models: dimensionless units, far fewer molecules than a flask (thousands against about
  10<sup>20</sup>), a well-mixed pot, and rates chosen or fitted, not measured.
- The mirror race is Frank's toy network, not the Soai mechanism and not the origin of life. Its spikes are not what a
  flask does; real runs ended with a partial lead of one hand (15-91% excess).
- The Soai toy is one published toy model of several; the real mechanism is still debated. Its K and turnovers per
  round are fitted to Soai's first two runs.
- Kagan's curve is the textbook ML2 model; real systems vary.
- No lab procedure, reagent, quantity or condition appears anywhere; the reaction is named, nothing more.

## Test it

Needs Node 18+ and `playwright-core` with a Chromium build (showtime installs both under `~/.showtime`), and
Python 3 with pytest; Pillow is optional (it shrinks the screenshots). See `requirements.txt`.

```bash
python3 -m pytest -q tests                  # build_data checks + the browser check (about 40 s)
PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH=~/.showtime/browsers node tests/test_page.mjs --shots --og   # also screenshots and og.jpg
cd .. && python3 -m page.run_all            # rebuild data.js, check, screenshots and og.jpg in one go
```

`test_build_data.py` has exact checks: the pie is exact arithmetic (0.75<sup>2</sup> = 56.25%, and so on, 90 - 10 = 80);
the inlined `kagan.js` gives 0.8 at 75:25 with K = 4, g = 0, the straight line for g = 1, 2x/(1 + x<sup>2</sup>) for
K = 4, g = 0, and 1 for K -> infinity; the binomial test against hand-computed values; L-alanine reads (S) and
D-alanine (R) from their coordinates (signed volume, with a hand-made centre as a check of the rule), and PubChem's two
conformers are mirror images through z = 0.

The browser check opens `index.html` from `file://` at 360, 768 and 1440 px in light and dark themes (and once with
reduced motion), stacks the molecules, makes every guess (step 1 by keyboard only), reveals every answer, moves every
slider, runs the live race with antagonism on and off, and checks: the reveal waits for a guess; each answer, its
label and its caveat appear; the Kagan curve at 75:25, K = 4, g = 0 reads 80% (and 50% with g = 1); the live race ends
with one hand only when antagonism is on; Soai's series and the 19/18, 15-91%, 27/27 panel are there; no unsourced "four nines" figure;
every chart has a title and a description; no sideways scrolling; no console errors; no network requests; page +
data under 1.5 MB; share tags present and no canonical link. It also checks that a copy with every input final hides
the "Preliminary" box and that copies without the mirror-race or Kagan results say so and still have no errors.
