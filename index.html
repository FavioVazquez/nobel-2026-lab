Medicine: the light switch

Physiology or Medicine 2026

Play · 2:21 · stops at 3 questions

The story in one line

In 1999 Francis Crick wrote that light would be the ideal signal for switching neurons, then called the idea “rather far-fetched”.

On 5 October 2026 the prize went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics” (press release).

Physics: a telescope made of ice

Physics 2026

Play · 2:37 · stops at 3 questions

The story in one line

About 65 billion neutrinos from the Sun pass through your little fingernail every second, and almost none of them touch anything. So how do you catch one?

On 6 October 2026 the prize went to Francis Halzen “for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin” (press release).

Chemistry: one hand wins

Chemistry 2026

Play · 2:30

The story in one line

Many molecules come in two mirror forms, like your hands, and life uses only one. How can a reaction choose a hand?

On 7 October 2026 the prize went to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai “for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis” (press release).

Literature: what survives

Literature 2026

Play · 2:29

The story in one line

Sappho wrote an estimated 9,000 to 10,000 lines. About 650 survive, and only one poem is certainly complete.

On 8 October 2026 the prize went to the Canadian author Anne Carson “for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature” (press release).

What is real, and what is a toy

Real

  • The prizes, the people, the dates and the papers. Every sentence is in a claims ledger with its source.
  • Numbers quoted from published papers, each with a link.
  • The published parameters the models start from, and IceCube's public event data (the 6 PeV replay).
  • For Literature, the ancient texts as public-domain editions printed them, byte for byte, each with its page.
  • For Peace, the court record from the judgments, and the open conflict data of the Uppsala Conflict Data Program as UCDP coded them.

A toy

  • The models: light, heat and nerve cells for Medicine; light in ice, a telescope fit and event rates for Physics; mirror-image molecules that pair up, copy themselves and race for Chemistry. Textbook ideas with simplified settings, every simplification listed.
  • For Literature, our own counts and drawings: letters and words counted in old editions, a hand alignment of six versions of one poem (our rough count, not a quality score), and estimates drawn as dots.
  • For Peace, our groupings and clocks on the UCDP data: agreement and ceasefire counted together, "clear outcome", the clock that starts at the first quiet year. Correlation, not cause.
  • Their outputs are for learning. They are not checked against experiments and must not be used to plan an experiment, a device or a treatment.
